    The five moves

    • 1

      It has a start

      Wander is entered on purpose, next to “Walks joining your streets” — two peers, not a step and a not-step. Ambiguity you chose is anticipation; ambiguity you defaulted into is doubt.

    • 2

      The cloud breathes

      Only streets within reach warm up; the ones behind you cool into a walked trace. Everything else stays a faint dotted hint, so you can feel there is more without being told where. This is the move that stops the map going silent — and it costs you nothing, the GPS watch in app.jsx already publishes the fixes.

    • 3

      It answers only the question in front of you

      No route, no turn-by-turn — but at a fork, two photos and two words. Local, timely, and ignorable: “neither — keep wandering” has to be a real option or it isn’t under-navigation, it’s navigation with fewer words.

    • 4

      It can end

      Distance, streets, and how many were new to you. Without a terminal state there is no way to have wandered correctly, and an experience you can’t complete reads as one you got stuck in.

    • 5

      The exit stays visible

      “Give me a route” never disappears. Counter-intuitively this should increase tolerance for ambiguity — worth measuring as its own condition.

    As a study condition. These three states are separable and you can run them against each other with one prototype: defined route (today’s route view) · responsive wander (this) · static wander (today’s yellow). If the static one gets rated broken and the responsive one doesn’t, you’ve shown that the malfunction reading comes from unresponsiveness, not from vagueness — which is the claim worth publishing, because it contradicts the usual framing of guidance as a single dial.
